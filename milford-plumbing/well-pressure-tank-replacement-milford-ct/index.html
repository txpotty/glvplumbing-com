    Flex-Lite Pressure Tank Replacement in Milford

    Flex-Lite composite pressure tanks are an good option for those seeking a lightweight, corrosion-resistant alternative to traditional steel tanks. They are especially well-suited for homes with aggressive water conditions that might accelerate corrosion in metal tanks. GLV Plumbing carefully replaces old or failing tanks with Flex-Lite models, ensuring a precise fit and proper connection to your existing well system.     Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Milford

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A simple test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and verify the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.

    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Milford

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the usable water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the most effective ways to extend pump life.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Milford, CT

    Diaphragm tanks like the Amtrol Well-X-Trol use a fixed membrane that separates air above from water below. They are tough, but the steel shell can rust through at the base after years of condensation in a hot, damp equipment space. We replace corroded diaphragm tanks and check the stand and fittings under them. The new tank is leveled and supported so it does not strain the piping. This restores steady pressure to every faucet.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Milford

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and choke flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives reliable readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in how well the system holds up.
