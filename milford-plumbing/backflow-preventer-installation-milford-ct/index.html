    Reduced Pressure Zone Assembly Installation in Milford, CT

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of protection and are required for high hazard connections like commercial kitchens, medical facilities, chemical injection and car washes. They include two independent check valves with a relief valve that dumps water if either check fails. Because the relief valve can discharge, we plan for drainage and install the assembly above grade where it can be tested easily. We carry trusted brands like Watts, Febco, Wilkins and Apollo.

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Device Installation in Milford, CT

    For high-hazard applications in Milford, CT where toxic substances could contaminate the water supply, a reduced pressure zone, or RPZ, device is usually required. RPZ backflow preventers offer the highest level of protection, using a relief valve to discharge water if the pressure drops. GLV Plumbing is experienced with RPZ installation, making sure all components are correctly sized, positioned and set up according to state and local plumbing codes. Proper installation is critical for effective operation.

    Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in Milford

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules allow. We size them to avoid too much pressure loss on your system.

    Backflow Testing and Certification in Milford

    Most water providers require testable backflow assemblies to be certified at installation and then annually. Our certified testers check each valve's opening point, relief valve operation and differential pressure. If a device fails, we can repair or rebuild it on the spot with genuine parts.     Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Milford

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Milford, CT usually ranges from $400 to $1,500, depending on the type of device, its size and the complexity of the installation. A basic residential PVB for an irrigation system is generally on the lower end, while a commercial-grade RPZ assembly costs more because of material and labor requirements.

    An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone device, provides the highest level of protection and is used for high-hazard applications where contaminants could be toxic. A DCVA, or double check valve assembly, is used for low-hazard applications where contaminants are non-toxic. The choice comes down to the risk of contamination at the cross-connection point.

    Backflow preventers are usually installed as a preventive measure or because code requires them. Signs of possible cross-contamination include discolored or foul-smelling tap water, unexplained drops in water pressure or visible dirty water entering a clean water source, though these can also point to broader plumbing issues as well.

    We commonly install high-quality backflow preventers from trusted brands such as Watts, Wilkins by Zurn, Apollo and Conbraco. Our plumbers are experienced with a wide range of devices and can recommend the right option for your application and budget.

    Constant dripping or discharge from an RPZ relief valve, low pressure downstream and a failed annual test are common signs. Visible corrosion or a cracked body from freezing also means service is needed.

    In Milford, most backflow preventers must be tested once a year by a certified backflow tester to confirm they are working correctly and providing adequate protection. This is a separate service from installation but is crucial for ongoing compliance and safety.
