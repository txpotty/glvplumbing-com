    Pipe Bursting in West Hartford, CT

    Pipe bursting is used when the old pipe is too damaged to line or needs a larger diameter. A bursting head is pulled through the old pipe, fracturing it outward while a new HDPE pipe follows behind. The new pipe is fused into one continuous length with no joints. Bursting needs an entry and exit pit but avoids digging the full length.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in West Hartford

    The cost of trenchless pipe repair in West Hartford varies significantly depending on the pipe's length, diameter, type of damage, and the specific trenchless method used, such as CIPP lining versus pipe bursting. Generally, you can expect costs to range from $80 to $250 per linear foot for residential projects. A camera inspection is essential for an accurate estimate from GLV Plumbing.

    Most common pipe materials, including cast iron, clay, PVC, and concrete, can be repaired using trenchless methods. However, the condition of the existing pipe is a factor. If a pipe is completely collapsed or badly misaligned, pipe bursting might be a more suitable trenchless option than lining. Our inspection will identify the best approach.

    We use liners and resins from established manufacturers such as Perma-Liner and similar systems rated for sewer use. We choose materials based on pipe size and condition.

    CIPP (cured-in-place pipe) lining involves inserting a new liner inside the existing pipe to create a pipe within a pipe. Pipe bursting, on the other hand, involves breaking apart the old pipe while pulling a new pipe into its place, resulting in a completely new pipe.

    Lining reduces the inside diameter slightly, but the smooth surface usually improves flow. Pipe bursting can maintain or increase the diameter.

    Many cities require a permit for sewer line replacement, including trenchless work. We check local requirements and obtain permits when needed.

    Trenchless pipe repairs, particularly CIPP lining, are designed to be extremely durable and long-lasting. The new pipe created inside the old one can have a lifespan of 50 years or more, often exceeding the expected life of the original piping. These repairs are resistant to corrosion, root intrusion, and chemical damage.
