    Pedestal Sump Pumps in West Hartford

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a good fit for narrow pits in older homes. They typically last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we generally recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in West Hartford, CT

    Submersible sump pumps sit directly in the sump pit, submerged in water, which makes them quieter and less intrusive. They are strong and effective for basements that take on a lot of water. Our team in West Hartford, CT will install your submersible pump with precision, making sure of proper sealing, discharge piping and electrical connections for quiet, reliable operation, which matters most in finished basements.

    Sump Pump Basin and Discharge Line Installation in West Hartford

    A complete sump pump system requires a properly installed basin and discharge line. We handle the excavation and placement of the sump basin, setting it at the lowest point of your basement floor so it collects water effectively. We also install the discharge line, ensuring it is correctly sloped, sealed and routed away from your foundation to a suitable drainage area, so water does not re-enter your West Hartford property.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in West Hartford

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by locating the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in West Hartford

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, designed to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.

    We routinely install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne because they offer cast iron housings, reliable switches and good parts support. We will match the brand and model to your pit size and budget.

    We install a wide range of well-built, reliable sump pump brands, including Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps. We can help you choose the best brand and model for your budget and water management needs in your West Hartford home, considering horsepower and features.

    Plumbing work in Connecticut follows the state building code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code, and plumbers must be licensed by the state. In many areas, a plumbing permit is required for new sump pump installations or significant replacements so the work meets local building codes. GLV Plumbing is familiar with local permitting requirements in West Hartford and can pull the necessary permits so your installation is compliant.

    Manufacturer warranties usually range from 3 to 5 years on quality cast iron pumps. We also back our installation labor, and we will walk you through the exact coverage for the model you choose.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump every 2 to 3 months, especially before and after the rainy season. Simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit to make sure the pump activates, drains the water and shuts off properly. Regular testing helps identify issues early.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is usually not allowed by local codes.
