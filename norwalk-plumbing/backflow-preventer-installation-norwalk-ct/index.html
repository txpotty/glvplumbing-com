    Irrigation Backflow Preventer Installation in Norwalk

    Landscape irrigation is the most common reason homeowners need a backflow device. Fertilizer, herbicides and stagnant water in sprinkler and drip lines can be siphoned into your drinking water if pressure drops. We install pressure vacuum breakers or double check assemblies based on local requirements and the layout of your system. Above-ground installs are set at least 12 inches above the highest sprinkler head when a PVB is used.

    Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in Norwalk

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules permit. We size them to avoid excessive pressure loss on your system.

    Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA) Installation in Norwalk

    The double check valve assembly, or DCVA, is a common backflow preventer for low-hazard applications where the consequences of backflow are not severe. In Norwalk, DCVAs are frequently installed on fire sprinkler systems or irrigation lines. Our certified plumbers precisely install these devices, making sure both check valves operate correctly and are properly sealed to prevent backpressure or back-siphonage into the main water supply.

    Pressure Vacuum Breaker (PVB) Installation in Norwalk, CT

    For low-hazard applications, such as lawn irrigation systems in Norwalk, CT, a pressure vacuum breaker, or PVB, is a popular and cost-effective backflow prevention device. PVBs protect against back-siphonage by letting air into the system when pressure drops. Our certified plumbers carefully install PVBs above the highest point of downstream piping, as code requires, to maintain effective protection. We also check for proper clearance and drainage.

    Backflow Enclosures and Freeze Protection in Norwalk, CT

    Above-ground assemblies sit in direct sun in summer and can freeze on cold nights. Frozen pipes are a regular winter risk in Connecticut, especially lines in exterior walls, crawlspaces, and unheated porches or garages. We install insulated enclosures, freeze blankets or heated cabinets based on your local climate. Enclosures also discourage theft, since brass backflow assemblies are a common target for scrap thieves. We choose enclosures with enough room for testing and service.
