    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Norwalk

    Underground water line leaks are often hard to detect until serious damage has occurred. Common signs include sudden spikes in your water bill, damp spots in your yard, or a noticeable drop in water pressure. Our team uses acoustic leak detection and ground-penetrating radar to precisely locate these hidden leaks in Norwalk, reducing the need for extensive excavation. We repair breaks caused by shifting soil, tree root intrusion, or aging pipes, restoring your water service efficiently.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Norwalk, CT

    A single leak on a newer copper or PEX line is usually a simple spot repair. Repeat leaks, galvanized pipe or a line older than about 40 years usually justify full replacement. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars, while a full replacement costs more but resets the clock for decades. We show you the condition of the pipe we expose so you can make the call with real information.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Norwalk, CT

    A full break can flood a yard, wash out soil under walkways and leave your home without water. Our emergency crews shut off the supply, locate the break and set up a temporary bypass when the repair will take more than a few hours. Most emergency breaks are repaired on the same day. We also document the damage with photos for your insurance or HOA.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Norwalk

    Most main line leaks show up as a soggy strip of yard, a sudden jump in your water bill or a meter dial that moves with every fixture off. We verify the leak by isolating the house shutoff and watching the meter. Then we use ground microphones to find the exact spot. A single section is cut out and replaced with repair couplings or a new length of pipe, and the line is pressure tested before we backfill.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Norwalk, CT

    Many homes built before the 1970s still have galvanized steel service lines that rust from the inside. Public water in most of Connecticut runs soft to moderately hard, and soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper plumbing. Hard water speeds up scale buildup, which restricts flow and causes rust-colored water. Patching galvanized pipe seldom holds up because the surrounding pipe is just as corroded. We usually recommend replacing the full run from meter to house with copper or PEX, which restores pressure and stops repeat leaks.
