    New Construction Water Service Lines in New Britain

    New homes, guest houses and additions need a correctly sized service line from the meter to the building. We review the plans, confirm meter size with your water provider and choose a line diameter that matches the fixture load. Most homes need a 3/4 inch or 1 inch line, while larger homes with pools and big irrigation systems often benefit from 1 1/4 inch. We coordinate the rough-in timing with your builder so the line is ready when the inspector arrives.

    Copper Water Line Installation in New Britain, CT

    Copper has been a trusted material for water lines for decades, known for its longevity, antimicrobial properties and resistance to high temperatures and pressure. GLV Plumbing provides professional copper water line installation in New Britain, CT, for homeowners who prefer its long history.     Commercial Water Service Installation in New Britain

    Restaurants, retail suites and small commercial buildings need service lines sized for higher demand and often require backflow protection at the meter. We install larger diameter copper or HDPE lines and coordinate with your water utility on tap and meter requirements. Our plumbers schedule work to minimize downtime for tenants and customers. Inspection and backflow test reports are handled before the line goes into service.

    Water Line Installation Cost Comparison in New Britain, CT

    Cost depends mostly on run length, soil conditions and pipe material. A short PEX run through soft soil is the most affordable option, while long copper runs through rocky ground cost more in both labor and materials. Trenchless methods cost more per foot but can save thousands in concrete and landscape repair. We provide a written quote that breaks out trenching, pipe, fittings, permits and restoration.

    Upsizing Water Lines for Better Pressure in New Britain, CT

    Many homes built decades ago have a 3/4 inch line that cannot keep up with modern fixture loads, irrigation and pools. If your pressure drops when two showers run or the sprinklers are on, an undersized line may be the culprit. We test static and flowing pressure, then calculate whether a larger line will make a real difference. Upsizing is often paired with a new pressure reducing valve and main shutoff.
