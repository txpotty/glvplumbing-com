    Backflow prevention is essential because of changing water pressures, main breaks and fire flow that can drop pressure suddenly, and the potential for cross-connections from irrigation systems or industrial processes. These factors increase the risk of contaminated water entering the public supply, making a working backflow preventer essential.

    Most municipalities, including Norwich, require backflow preventers to be tested annually. This regular testing makes sure the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination, as required by local water authorities.

    An RPZ, or Reduced Pressure Zone assembly, offers the highest level of protection, suitable for high-hazard cross-connections. A DCVA, or Double Check Valve Assembly, provides moderate protection and is usually used for low to medium hazard situations, like irrigation systems. The choice depends on the application and local code rules.

    The lifespan of a backflow preventer usually ranges from 10 to 20 years, depending on the type, manufacturer, water quality, and maintenance. Regular testing and timely repairs can extend its life. However, certain components like rubber seals may need replacement sooner due to normal wear.
