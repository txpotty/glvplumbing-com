    Frequently Asked Questions About Urinal Installation in Norwich

    Replacing a urinal on existing lines usually costs $500 to $1,200 including a manual flush valve. New installs with carriers and new drain lines usually range from $1,500 to $3,500, and sensor valves add $250 to $600.

    Most standard urinal installations or replacements can be done within 2 to 4 hours. However, if new plumbing lines need to be run, or if there are significant structural modifications required, the process could take a full day or more.

    A washdown urinal uses a simple flushing action to rinse the bowl, relying on gravity and the slope of the drain. A siphonic urinal creates a siphonic action during flushing, which pulls waste more forcefully down the drain, offering more effective waste removal and usually better odor control.

    Switching from a 1 gpf urinal to a 0.125 gpf model saves most of the water per flush. In a busy restroom that adds up to tens of thousands of gallons a year.

    Flush urinals use a water flush to remove waste, while waterless urinals use a specialized trap system with a sealant liquid to prevent odors without water. Waterless urinals offer major water savings but require specific maintenance for their sealant cartridges. Both types offer effective waste disposal.

    Regular cleaning with appropriate restroom cleaners is essential. For waterless urinals, follow manufacturer guidelines for cartridge replacement. For flushed urinals, make sure the flush mechanism is working correctly and address any slow draining issues quickly to prevent blockages.

    GLV Plumbing installs a wide variety of reputable urinal brands, including American Standard, Kohler, Zurn, Sloan, and Toto. We are familiar with the specific requirements of most major manufacturers and can also recommend the best brand for your specific needs and budget.

    For commercial settings, look for durability, water efficiency (like low-flow or waterless models), easy maintenance, and ADA compliance if applicable. Brands like Sloan and Zurn are popular for their robust commercial-grade fixtures and flushometers.
