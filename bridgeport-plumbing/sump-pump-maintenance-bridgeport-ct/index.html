    The discharge line carries water away from your basement, and the check valve prevents pumped water from flowing back into the pit. We inspect these components for blockages, leaks or damage.     Check Valve and Discharge Line Service in Bridgeport, CT

    The check valve stops pumped water from falling back into the pit when the pump shuts off. A failed valve makes the pump run twice as often and wears out the motor. We test and replace check valves and inspect the discharge pipe for cracks and loose joints. We also make sure water exits away from the house so it does not soak into the soil around the slab or foundation.

    Pre-Storm Season Pump Testing in Bridgeport

    Nor'easters and tropical storms can bring heavy rain and coastal flooding to Connecticut, often cutting power to sump pumps right when they are needed.     Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Bridgeport, CT

    The float switch is the most common reason a sump pump fails to start. Tethered floats can tangle on the pump body, and vertical floats can stick from mineral crust or debris. We clean or replace the switch and set the on and off levels so the pump does not short cycle. Electronic sensor switches are an option for tight pits where floats get stuck.

    Float Switch Inspection and Adjustment in Bridgeport, CT

    The float switch is a critical component that tells your sump pump when to turn on and off. A stuck or improperly adjusted float switch can lead to the pump running continuously or failing to activate at all. During maintenance, we inspect the float switch for freedom of movement and proper calibration, making adjustments as needed to support reliable operation for your home in Bridgeport, CT.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Maintenance in Bridgeport

    The cost of sump pump maintenance in Bridgeport, CT typically ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the complexity of your system and any minor repairs needed. This usually includes inspection, cleaning and testing. Major repairs or part replacements would be quoted separately after diagnosis.

    Public water in most of Connecticut runs soft to moderately hard, and soft water can be mildly corrosive to older copper plumbing. Where groundwater is hard, mineral deposits can build up on the pump's impeller and in the pit, reducing efficiency and possibly causing clogs or damage over time. Regular maintenance helps limit these effects.

    Routine maintenance typically does not require a permit. However, if a significant repair involves altering the discharge line or replacing the entire unit, especially if electrical work is involved, a local permit might be necessary. We handle all permit requirements for you.

    Watch out for unusual noises, such as grinding or rattling, that point to debris or a failing motor. If the pump runs constantly, fails to turn on or gives off a foul odor from the pit, it's definitely time for a professional check. Visible rust or corrosion are also red flags.

    A standard sump pump maintenance appointment typically takes about 30 to 60 minutes. This time allows for a complete inspection, cleaning and testing of all components. If minor repairs are needed, the visit might run a little longer.

    Most local codes do not allow sump discharge into the sanitary sewer. We route discharge to the yard, a dry well or another approved location.

    If your primary sump pump fails during a power outage and you don't have a working battery backup or water-powered backup system, your basement is at risk of flooding. This shows the importance of maintaining backup systems.

    The best time is a few weeks before your wettest season begins. Homes that see snow may also want a fall check before winter storms and snowmelt.
