    Underground Water Line Leak Detection in Danbury

    Leaks in underground water lines, from your meter to your home, can go unnoticed for extended periods, wasting water and causing yard erosion. Signs include oddly green patches in your lawn, persistent wet spots or a constantly running water meter. GLV Plumbing employs ground-penetrating radar and tracer gas detection methods to accurately map out and pinpoint leaks in buried pipes, allowing for targeted excavation and repair rather than digging up your entire yard.

    Water Heater and Softener Leak Detection in Danbury

    Water heaters and softeners can leak at fittings, valves and drain lines. A softener stuck in regeneration can send water down the drain continuously, and a dripping relief valve on a water heater wastes water too. We check these units carefully and repair or replace failed parts. That often explains a mystery bill in homes that have water softeners.

    Yard and Outdoor Leak Detection in Danbury

    Leaks in outdoor plumbing, irrigation systems or main water lines buried in your yard can lead to wasted water and landscaping damage. We use ground-penetrating radar and tracer gas detection to locate buried pipes and identify leaks without extensive digging. Our approach minimizes disruption to your lawn and garden, preserving your property's appearance. Detecting these outdoor leaks early can also prevent erosion and potential foundation issues caused by saturated soil, a critical concern for homes in Danbury, CT.

    Main Service Line Leak Detection in Danbury, CT

    Toilet and Fixture Leak Detection in Danbury, CT

    Toilets are one of the most common sources of wasted water. A worn flapper or a fill valve that never shuts off can leak quietly around the clock. We use dye tests to find leaking toilets and check faucets, showers and supply lines for drips. These fixes are typically quick and save water right away.

    Pool Water Leak Detection in Danbury, CT

    Pools lose a lot of water to evaporation in summer heat, so a leak can hide behind normal loss. We use a bucket test to compare evaporation with actual water loss.
