    Heat Exchanger Descaling in New Haven

    The heat exchanger in a tankless heater is a coil of small tubes that water races through while the burner fires. Hard water drops calcium and magnesium on those hot surfaces, narrowing the passages over time. We connect a submersible pump and hoses to the service valves and circulate descaling solution for 45 to 60 minutes. Then we flush with fresh water to rinse the system completely. Restored flow means more stable temperatures and fewer shutdowns.

    Inlet Filter and Air Intake Cleaning in New Haven

    Every tankless unit has an inlet water screen that catches grit and scale flakes. Those screens can clog with sand and debris, especially after municipal line work. We pull and clean the screen and inspect the air intake filter, which can load up with dust and dryer lint. Clean filters keep the flow sensor reading correctly and the burner breathing properly.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Descaling in New Haven, CT

    Isolation Valve Kit Installation in New Haven, CT

We install a set of isolation valves with purge ports on the hot and cold lines so future maintenance takes minutes. The kit also lets you shut off water to the heater without closing the main.     Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Maintenance in New Haven

    The cost for tankless water heater maintenance in New Haven, CT typically ranges from $150 to $300, depending on the unit's age, condition and the extent of descaling required. This includes a full flush and inspection of critical components. More complex issues or additional repairs would be quoted on their own.

    Tankless water heater maintenance focuses mainly on descaling the heat exchanger and checking electronic components for efficiency. Hot water tank maintenance typically involves flushing sediment from the bottom of the tank, checking the anode rod for corrosion and inspecting the pressure relief valve. Both aim to prolong life but address different system specifics.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to several problems, including reduced heating efficiency, higher utility bills, decreased hot water output and eventually, costly breakdowns. Mineral buildup can severely restrict water flow and damage the heat exchanger, potentially leading to premature unit failure and expensive repairs or replacement.

    A typical tankless water heater maintenance service, including flushing and inspection, generally takes 1 to 2 hours. The exact time can vary depending on the unit's condition, the amount of scale buildup and whether any minor adjustments or repairs are needed during the visit.

    Common signs include reduced hot water flow, fluctuating water temperatures, odd noises like banging or popping, error codes displayed on the unit, or a noticeable increase in your energy bills. If you notice any of these symptoms, it's time to schedule a professional inspection and maintenance.

    If you have isolation valves, a pump and the right solution, a handy homeowner can do it. A pro visit also checks gas pressure, venting, sensors and condensate, which a DIY flush skips.

    Many manufacturers exclude scale damage from heat exchanger warranties. Regular, documented maintenance is the best way to keep your coverage intact.

    With regular descaling, many tankless heaters last 15 to 20 years. Neglected units in hard water can fail in under half that time.
