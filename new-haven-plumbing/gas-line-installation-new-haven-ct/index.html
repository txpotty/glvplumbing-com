    Gas Water Heater and Tankless Line Upgrades in New Haven, CT

    Tankless water heaters often need up to 199,000 BTU, far more than the 40,000 BTU a typical tank heater uses. Many existing 1/2 inch lines cannot supply that demand, which causes error codes and lukewarm water. We calculate the full house load and upsize the line to 3/4 inch or larger as needed. This upgrade is one of the most important steps in a successful tankless conversion.

    Gas Line Installation for Generators in New Haven

    When installing a whole-home generator, a dedicated gas line is necessary for continuous power. We provide professional gas line installation to connect your generator to your home's gas supply. Our team ensures the line is correctly sized for the generator's BTU requirements and installed to withstand local conditions, providing reliable backup power for your New Haven property during outages.

    Gas Range and Cooktop Line Installation in New Haven

    Switching from an electric range to gas is one of the most frequent reasons homeowners call us. Professional-style ranges can need 60,000 BTU or more, so we confirm the branch and main can handle the load. The appliance is then checked for proper burner flame and ignition.

    Outdoor Kitchen and BBQ Gas Lines in New Haven

    Built-in grills, side burners and pizza ovens are a popular part of backyard living.     Pool and Spa Heater Gas Lines in New Haven

    Pool heaters are among the largest gas appliances in a home, often rated at 250,000 to 400,000 BTU. They usually sit at the equipment pad far from the meter, so line size and length are crucial. We may recommend a larger main line or a meter upgrade from your gas utility. A properly sized line lets the heater reach its rated output and heat the water efficiently.

    CSST Flexible Gas Piping in New Haven, CT

    Corrugated stainless steel tubing installs faster than black iron and routes easily through attics and finished walls with fewer joints. It is a good option for remodels and additions where opening walls is costly. CSST must be bonded to the home's grounding system to reduce lightning damage risk.
